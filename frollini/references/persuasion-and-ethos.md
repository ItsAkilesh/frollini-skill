# Persuasion and ethos

Sources: Barrett, *Leadership Communication*, Ch. 1, and the CMU 17-603 lecture notes. Paraphrased.

## What persuasive communication is

Leadership communication is a deliberate transfer of meaning meant to move a person or group to act. It works when the writer connects with the reader, gets past whatever is in the way, and gives the reader a reason to act.

## Why messages fail

A message goes from a sender, through a medium, to a receiver, inside a context. It can break at any of those points:

- The message: muddled thinking, an unclear point, illogical structure, poor style.
- The delivery: the wrong medium, poor formatting, bad timing, the wrong messenger.
- The reader and context: no audience analysis, an offensive tone, cultural misreadings, a message that clashes with what the reader already believes.
- The sender: low credibility or doubtful ethics.

When a draft isn't working, go through this list.

## Three appeals

Aristotle named three ways to persuade: ethos (the writer's credibility), pathos (emotion), and logos (logic and evidence). Ethos carries the most weight. A reader who doesn't trust you won't weigh your logic.

## Building credibility in writing

Make the message believable. Keep it clear and logical, get all the facts first, be honest, and avoid careless errors. One wrong number can cost the whole document its credibility.

Show the right character. Know the subject, sound confident without overclaiming, and respect the reader's time.

Put the reader in the right frame of mind. Understand what they need and want, respect their context and culture, and frame the message for them in particular.

## Influence without authority

People influence others through different kinds of power:

| Power | Comes from |
|---|---|
| Coercive | The threat of punishment |
| Reward | The promise of benefits |
| Legitimate | A title or position |
| Referent | Personal appeal |
| Expert | Knowledge and skill |
| Information | Having information others need |
| Connection | Relationships and networks |

When you write to people who don't report to you, demands backfire. Rely on expertise, information, relationships, and shared goals. Show each reader what they gain.

## Ethics

Ethical standards come from law, contracts, professional and company codes, cultural norms, and personal values. Four common ways to judge a decision:

- By results: the most good for the most stakeholders.
- By duty: rights, rules, and fairness.
- By community norms: what the group expects of a person of good character.
- By conscience: what feels right.

A quick test that works across cultures is to treat others as you'd want to be treated. Another is to ask whether you'd be comfortable seeing the message quoted publicly. Integrity is the base of credibility.
