# Presentations

Sources: the CMU 17-603 lecture "Keys to Effective Presentation Skills," Toastmasters' "Giving Effective Feedback," and Barrett, Ch. 6–7. Paraphrased. More detail is in `textbook-ch1-7.md`.

## When to use slides

Use slides for long or detailed talks, or when a visual explains something faster than words. Skip them for short, informal, or small-group conversations where they add nothing.

## Four things that make a talk work

1. Know the material. Review it while you build the deck and again the night before. Research beyond your slides so you can handle questions, and predict what people will ask. Knowing the material is where confidence comes from.
2. Organize it. Tell them what you'll cover, cover it, then sum up. Open with the main argument, then move into supporting detail and finer detail before the close.
3. Make it readable. If the audience can't read a slide, they stop listening. Dress to match the formality of the setting.
4. Deliver it with confidence. Practice until the order and wording come naturally.

## Slide text

- Make each title a headline that states the takeaway. "Widget sales missed plan by 40%" beats "Q4 results by product category."
- Use two to four short bullets, not full sentences.
- Use one chart or table that proves the headline, or none.
- Use high-contrast colors, large fonts, and no clutter.
- Use animation only when it shows a sequence. No sound effects.
- Remember that slides get printed and forwarded, so each one has to make sense without you.

## Delivery

- Speak slowly and clearly.
- Don't read your slides.
- Know the room and the equipment.
- Make eye contact with several parts of the room in turn.
- Use notes only if you need them; practice removes the need.
- Hand out handouts after the talk, with room for notes.
- Always have a backup: a copy on USB or in the cloud, and printouts.

## Short impromptu talks

State your point in one sentence. Give two reasons or one short story. Restate the point. Start wrapping up before your time is up.

## Giving feedback

Feedback works when it is specific, prompt, and sincere.

- Say exactly what you saw.
- Speak for yourself: "I noticed..."
- Explain how the behavior affects the team.
- Recognize what went well and what improved since last time.
- Give one or two clear changes, not a long list.
- End on something positive and genuine.

When praising, be specific, say thanks, and encourage more of the same.
